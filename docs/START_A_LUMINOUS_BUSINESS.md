# Start a Luminous Business

**Purpose:** help an independent IT operator, small MSP, cooperative, or regional implementation partner launch a repeatable service around the Luminous Platform without needing to build the platform, run every repository, or become a Rust developer.

**Status:** launch playbook and templates. This does not grant partner accreditation, guarantee demand, or assert that unqualified platform capabilities are available.

## The principle: one platform, many repeatable businesses

Do not make every entrepreneur assemble Symthaea, Mycelix, Nixward, Xenia, and Sovereign Ops from source.

Give them one clear path:

1. Choose a customer and one service offer.
2. Use the starter pack's scope, intake, delivery, and evidence templates.
3. Deliver that service with tools and capabilities that are actually qualified today.
4. Produce a professional report and agree a recurring follow-up.
5. Add platform automation only when the exact workflow and target environment have passed their qualification gate.

The business must be useful before it has access to AI-driven decisions or privileged automation.

## Who can start

### Independent operator / consultant

For a capable systems administrator, security practitioner, or infrastructure engineer who wants to sell a narrow outcome. They can begin with an assessment and runbook service and use customer-approved exports and established tools. No custom platform coding is required for the manual service version.

### Existing MSP / IT services firm

For a provider that already maintains customer relationships and a service desk. Begin beside the existing PSA/RMM stack, not by requiring customers to rip it out. Use the incident-to-evidence workflow to improve documentation, recovery confidence, and follow-through. Live integrations remain out of scope until the specific connector is authenticated and qualified.

### Regional delivery partner

For a consultancy, co-op, or local IT shop that wants to install and support self-hosted systems. Partner delivery becomes repeatable through a compatibility matrix, deployment profile, implementation runbook, training, and clear escalation boundary. Do not call a partner “certified” until there is a defined, assessed programme.

### Community or sector operator

For a cooperative, community network, research lab, or energy operator. Use the same platform concepts later, but do not reuse the MSP security/recovery playbook as if it were a safety-qualified energy or industrial control product.

## The first offer: Operational Resilience Baseline

Start with a short, fixed-scope service. It is designed to be deliverable with normal professional IT methods while the Luminous runtime continues to mature.

**Customer outcome:** a clear picture of one agreed environment's most important operational risks, named owners, a practical recovery/change plan, and a list of items that are demonstrated, only documented, or still unproven.

### Included

- One customer organization and one agreed service/environment.
- Kickoff and boundary confirmation.
- Customer-approved inventory and dependency review.
- Review of access ownership, update/backup practices, critical-service dependencies, and existing incident/change records.
- One tabletop or non-destructive scenario walkthrough.
- A prioritized remediation list with owner, target date, and evidence required to close.
- An operator-readable report and a customer handoff meeting.
- An explicit list of excluded systems and evidence limitations.

### Not included by default

- Penetration testing, incident response, forensic acquisition, or legal/regulatory compliance certification.
- Production configuration changes, account resets, destructive restore tests, or privileged remote action.
- Handling customer passwords, private signing keys, raw personal data, or production secrets in the Luminous prototype.
- Any claim of ransomware immunity or independently certified resilience.

Expand scope only with a separate agreement, the necessary expertise, a safe test environment, customer authorization, and a qualified method.

## Productized service ladder

Keep the menu short. A customer should understand what each offer buys.

| Offer | Deliverable | When to sell it |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Baseline Sprint** | Inventory boundary, prioritized risk/remediation plan, recovery gaps, owner handoff | First engagement; lowest operational authority |
| **2. Recovery Exercise** | A bounded test plan, restore/rebuild evidence, measured result and limitations | Only when an appropriate isolated recovery environment and competent operator exist |
| **3. Monthly Operations Assurance** | Scheduled change review, evidence hygiene, drift/recovery checks, remediation tracking | After the customer agrees on scope, support hours, escalation, and measurable outcomes |
| **4. Platform Integration** | One named PSA/monitoring/deployment adapter with a compatibility and test report | Only after the specific adapter is implemented and qualified |

The first service may be delivered manually. Do not describe manual evidence collection as an automated Luminous capability. Do not sell offers 2–4 as automated platform features until their precise technical gates are met.

## How to price without guessing

Do not invent one global price. Calculate a local price floor and test it with real buyers.

`Minimum quote = delivery labour + preparation + travel + tooling/hosting + insurance/risk allowance + overhead + applicable taxes/fees`

For a recurring service, include the expected monthly labour, escalation/response coverage, tooling, reporting, and the cost of keeping skills and integrations current. State what is excluded, what counts as out-of-scope work, and how emergency work is authorized and billed.

A low first-customer price may be reasonable for a tightly bounded design engagement, but record the real time and cost so that it does not become an unprofitable permanent price. Validate willingness to pay before building a billing system or promising a SaaS model.

## The customer journey

1. **Qualify:** identify one service the customer cares about, the accountable decision-maker, and what data/access can be provided safely.
2. **Scope:** sign the work statement, agree data handling, target boundaries, acceptance criteria, exclusions, and escalation route.
3. **Baseline:** use customer-approved sources; keep findings linked to their source and observation date.
4. **Review:** explain evidence strength—observed, documented, simulated, tested, or unproven—without blending them into a green score.
5. **Deliver:** issue the report, assign remediation owners, and agree a follow-up date.
6. **Renew:** offer a review cadence based on the customer's actual risks and capacity, not a forced subscription.
7. **Automate carefully:** replace a manual step only when a corresponding adapter has tests, authority boundaries, data handling, failure behavior, and a supported environment.

## First 30 days

### Days 1–5: set up the service business

- Choose a customer niche and a single offer.
- Pick a business name and domain after local name/trademark checks.
- Check local business registration, tax/VAT/sales tax, licensing, privacy, recordkeeping, insurance, and contracting requirements with the relevant official agencies or qualified adviser.
- Open a separate business account where appropriate and establish basic bookkeeping.
- Write a plain-language service description, exclusions, data handling statement, and escalation policy.
- Do not advertise Luminous partner certification unless it exists and has been awarded.

### Days 6–10: make delivery repeatable

- Complete the included service templates in this directory.
- Define the minimum inputs and which data must never be collected.
- Rehearse the report and handoff using synthetic data.
- Create a checklist that records the exact evidence sources and unverified assumptions.
- Build a simple way to record time spent, direct costs, and customer feedback.

### Days 11–20: secure a first design customer

- Speak to a small set of relevant businesses and ask about the last operational failure, the cost of disruption, how they prove recovery works, and who owns the remediation.
- Offer a tightly scoped paid or explicitly discounted baseline—not a promise that the platform is already production-qualified.
- Confirm the customer understands what will and will not be tested.
- Avoid collecting privileged credentials just to make the engagement look more automated.

### Days 21–30: deliver and measure

- Deliver the agreed report and review it with the customer.
- Measure actual labour, elapsed time, blocked evidence, remediation acceptance, and willingness to pay.
- Ask whether the customer would pay to repeat the service or refer another customer.
- Change the offer based on evidence, not the number of features built.
- Do not scale until one delivery can be repeated without undocumented founder-only knowledge.

## What the Luminous Platform should provide to every business starter

The platform should make the repeatable work easy and leave professional judgment where it belongs:

- **Start page:** pick a business profile and guided first offer.
- **Starter pack:** scope, intake, delivery checklist, report and follow-up template.
- **Deployment advisor:** choose a supported profile; explain prerequisites and limitations before install.
- **Customer workspace:** tenant-scoped records and evidence references, not a store for customer secrets.
- **Workflow states:** proposed, approved, executing, verified, unresolved, or blocked; never infer “verified” from a suggestion or mock.
- **Evidence export:** portable report with source, revision, timestamps, limitations and integrity information.
- **Adapter catalogue:** supported versions, configuration, known limits, security boundary, test status and upgrade path.
- **Business operations:** service templates, time/cost tracking, renewal reminders and optional invoicing integration—not a mandatory proprietary billing system.
- **Learn and support:** plain-language explanations, local service-provider discovery, runbooks, and escalation boundaries.

The on-ramp should work without a Mycelix identity, Symthaea model, central cloud account, or paid subscription unless a particular feature explicitly needs it. These systems can be added later and must remain replaceable.

## Partner model

Use a progressive model rather than a gatekeeping franchise:

- **Community implementer:** uses the public templates and stays within declared capabilities.
- **Supported delivery partner:** completes technical training, demonstrates the workflow on a test environment, publishes supported scope, and agrees to a support/security process.
- **Qualified specialist:** independently assessed for a defined capability such as a recovery exercise or a specific adapter. Scope and expiry of qualification are explicit.

A status applies only to the capability assessed. It does not make every action by the partner or every installation safe.

## 90-day evidence goals

The initial cohort should aim to produce evidence, not just sign-ups:

- A small set of completed, in-scope engagements.
- Repeatable delivery reports with source-linked evidence and explicit unknowns.
- Measured hours, direct costs, rework, and gross margin per offer.
- Customer-confirmed value and willingness to renew or refer.
- A list of recurring manual steps that merit automation.
- No unsupported claims of compliance, automated recovery, or independent qualification.

If the service doesn't create enough customer value to justify its cost, revise or retire it. Do not build increasingly complex platform modules to defend an offer that customers don't need.

## Legal and cybersecurity references

These references provide orientation, not jurisdiction-specific legal advice:

- [U.S. Small Business Administration: Launch your business](https://www.sba.gov/business-guide/launch-your-business) — location, structure, name, registration, tax IDs, licences/permits, banking and insurance.
- [NIST SP 1300: Cybersecurity Framework 2.0 Small Business Quick-Start Guide](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/1300/final) — adaptable baseline for small businesses.
- [NIST Small Business Cybersecurity Corner](https://www.nist.gov/itl/smallbusinesscyber) — freely available small-business cybersecurity guidance.

A founder must use the official business, tax, privacy and licensing sources for their jurisdiction. The U.S. references above are not applicable law for every country.

## Success criterion

A competent operator should be able to understand the offer, quote it, deliver it safely, report what was actually proven, and decide whether to renew—without understanding every internal Luminous repository or surrendering customer control to a hosted platform.
