# Sources and competitive baseline

Research snapshot: **2026-11-10**. Links are primary publisher, standards-body, laboratory,
manufacturer or South African utility sources where available. This list is a starting evidence
corpus, not a complete standards review.

## Solid-state transformer and modular DC/DC

1. **IEEE Open Journal of Power Electronics**, “A Review of DC-DC Converters for Modular Solid-State Transformers: Challenges, Comparative Analysis, and Design,” Volume 7, pp. 1846–1864, published 3 June 2026. DOI: [10.1109/OJPEL.2026.3699737](https://doi.org/10.1109/OJPEL.2026.3699737). Reports experimental validation of three PEBB topology options and 98.55% peak efficiency in its case study. The figure is a literature anchor, not an equivalent-performance guarantee for PEBB-LV-01.
2. **IEEE / ScienceDirect**, “Solid state transformer in energy conversion and distribution: a structured design framework, four-dimensional taxonomy, control strategies, and deployment roadmap,” 2026. [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S259017452600437X). Useful for SST system taxonomy, topology-selection methodology, maturity and deployment barriers.
3. **IEEE**, “Qualification Challenges of Solid-State Transformers: Standards, Medium Voltage Power Semiconductors, and Auxiliary Circuits,” IPEC-Nagasaki 2026. [IEEE Xplore](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/abstract/document/11597371). Highlights medium-voltage semiconductor, medium-frequency transformer and auxiliary-circuit qualification as deployment barriers.
4. **IEEE**, “Insulation Challenges in Medium-Voltage High-Frequency Transformers for Solid-State Transformers: A Review of Mechanisms, Diagnostics, and Mitigation,” early access, 18 August 2026. [IEEE Xplore](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/abstract/document/11658780). Covers coupled dv/dt, frequency, temperature and insulation degradation / partial discharge issues.

## Semiconductor and component reliability

5. “Reliability Challenges, Models, and Physics of Silicon Carbide and Gallium Nitride Power Devices,” Energies, 18(5), 1046, 21 February 2025. [Open access article](https://www.mdpi.com/1996-1073/18/5/1046). Reviews thermal degradation, defect propagation, charge trapping and robustness testing.
6. “Mission-profile based reliability framework for medium-frequency transformers,” Power Electronic Devices and Components, Volume 12, December 2025, 100114. [DOI 10.1016/j.pedc.2025.100114](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.pedc.2025.100114). Uses mission-profile stress, insulation wear-out, partial-discharge-related effects and Monte Carlo uncertainty in a 170 kW, 15 kHz DAB case study.

## Grid-forming and hardware-in-the-loop

9. **Energy Systems Integration Group**, “Testing the Performance of Grid-Forming Resources: Test Methods and Performance Metrics for Evaluating the Voltage Source Behavior of Grid-Forming Resources,” September 2025. [Report page](https://www.esig.energy/reports-briefs/testing-the-performance-of-grid-forming-resources/). Important future microgrid benchmark source.
10. **National Laboratory of the Rockies**, [Grid Simulation and Power Hardware-in-the-Loop](https://www.nlr.gov/grid/simulation-phil). Describes 1.08 MVA and 7 MVA grid simulators and real-time PHIL capabilities.
11. **National Laboratory of the Rockies**, “Performance Evaluation of a Single-Phase Grid-Forming Inverter Through Hardware Experiments,” 2024. [Publication record](https://research-hub.nlr.gov/en/publications/performance-evaluation-of-a-single-phase-grid-forming-inverter-th-2/). 12. **IEEE Standards Association**, [P2800.1 project](https://standards.ieee.org/ieee/2800.1/12387/). Project for recommended practice on capabilities and performance of grid-forming IBR/CBR equipment; the cited page identifies it as an active project, not a published final standard.

## Standards and South African context

13. **IEC**, [IEC 62909-1:2025 — Bi-directional grid-connected power converters, Part 1: General and safety requirements](https://webstore.iec.ch/en/publication/69114). Its scope explicitly excludes certain PV, UPS and EV charging equipment; product-specific standards must be mapped separately.
14. **SSEG / NRS**, [NRS 097-2-1:2024 — Grid interconnection of embedded generation, Part 2, Section 1: Utility interface](https://www.sseg.org.za/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/NRS-097-2-1-Published-2024.pdf). South African small-scale embedded-generation utility-interface context.
15. **Eskom Distribution**, [Small-scale embedded generators](https://www.eskom.co.za/distribution/small-scale-embedded-generators/). Current registration and documentation information for relevant Eskom-connected SSEG installations.
16. **Eskom**, [EGI Report, March 2026](https://www.eskom.co.za/distribution/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/EGI-Report-March-2026_RP_240-153339608.pdf). Describes NRS 097-2-1 compliance-verification pathways including accredited third-party type-test evidence.

## 500 W converter research and loss-model validation

23. Wang, Hou, Zhao, Wu, Xie and Wang, “Parameter optimization design of bidirectional CLLC resonant converter under variable frequency control,” *Electric Power Systems Research*, Volume 255, Article 112834, June 2026, DOI [10.1016/j.epsr.2026.112834](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.epsr.2026.112834). The abstract and indexed paper text report an FHA-based resonant design method, soft-switching pre-design constraints, and a 500 W laboratory prototype with ZVS and 97.6% peak forward-transfer efficiency. The abstract does not establish matched reverse efficiency or our full operating range.
24. Wu, Zhao and Gao, “Verification of the DAB loss model at different frequencies,” 2025 IEEE 20th Conference on Industrial Electronics and Applications, DOI [10.1109/ICIEA65512.2025.11148703](https://doi.org/10.1109/ICIEA65512.2025.11148703). The indexed abstract describes MOSFET/diode conduction and switching, transformer core and inductor copper loss, SPS/EPS, 80–180 kHz and reported model/experiment deviation below 5 W across the tested scenarios. Its operating conditions are not a PEBB-LV-01 match; use it as a model-correlation methodology reference.
25. **MPS**, [48V Datacenter Solutions, 2025 brochure](https://media.monolithicpower.com/mps_cms_document/d/a/datacenter_solutions_2025_-_digital.pdf). The indexed 2025 table lists MPC10106-B-54-0500 with 40–60 V input, 8:1 ratio, 500 W-class output, 3,019 W/in³ and 97.65% peak efficiency. This is a non-isolated LLC first-stage module, not a bidirectional isolated converter.
26. Tu et al., “A Mode-Switching Four-Degree-of-Freedom Variable-Frequency Modulation Strategy for Dual-Active-Bridge Microinverters,” *Electronics*, 2026, DOI [10.3390/electronics15153256](https://doi.org/10.3390/electronics15153256). The indexed paper reports a 500 W grid-connected DAB microinverter prototype, 97.2% peak at 300 W, 96.5% at 500 W and 2.8% grid-current THD at 500 W. This is an AC grid-conversion/modulation reference, not a DC/DC PEBB comparison.
27. **ELSİS**, [BIDIR official product page](https://www.elsis.com.tr/en/products/bidir-module) and [official datasheet PDF](https://www.elsis.com.tr/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/BIDIR_DCDC_MODULE_DATASHEET.pdf). The website claims >97% and the datasheet's detailed features state >97% at full rated load current, while its cover summary states >95%. Preserve that discrepancy until the supplier provides a defined test curve and boundary.

28. Nkembi, Santoro, Delmonte and Cova, “Experimental Characterization and Validation of a PLECS-Based Hardware-in-the-Loop (HIL) Model of a Dual Active Bridge (DAB) Converter,” *Energies*, 19(2), 563, 22 January 2026, DOI [10.3390/en19020563](https://doi.org/10.3390/en19020563). Section 2 provides the ideal SPS power-transfer equation and equivalent referred inductance; the experimental section reports a 100 kHz, 1:1, 10 µH DAB HIL setup and tests equal 10/20/30 V plus 30→42 V. The paper also discusses loss of ZVS / increased circulating current and stress away from unity conversion ratio, especially at light load. It is a model/method reference, not a 500 W performance comparator.

29. Huang, Kong, Zhang, Ren and Li, “Design methodology for a bidirectional CLLC resonant converter based on T-type impedance-network constraints,” *IEICE Electronics Express*, Article 23.20260463, advance online publication 30 October 2026, DOI [10.1587/elex.23.20260463](https://doi.org/10.1587/elex.23.20260463). The full paper identifies how the secondary active bridge's complex equivalent AC impedance changes the T-network state and soft-switching condition. It derives a sampled-state feasible region intersecting switching-frequency, resonant-inductance-ratio, constant-power ZVS, unity-gain ZVS and maximum-gain constraints. Its 2 kW prototype uses 150–300 V input, 75–167 V output, 115 kHz resonant frequency and 75–150 kHz switching; measured efficiency reaches 95.7% forward (95.2% at rated forward load) and 95.4% reverse, with measured forward/reverse ZVS waveforms. This is a design-method reference, not a matched 500 W product or an efficiency SOTA claim.
30. Tao, Yao, Zhu, Zhang, Li and Chen, “Design and Implementation of a High-Gain Bidirectional CLLC Resonant Converter for Photovoltaic Energy Storage Systems,” *Electronics*, 15(21), 4918, published 23 October 2026, DOI [10.3390/electronics15214918](https://doi.org/10.3390/electronics15214918). Reports a 4.8 kW bidirectional CLLC PV-storage prototype with 97.45% peak charging efficiency and 96.66% peak discharging efficiency. It combines FHA gain, gain monotonicity, inductive input impedance, magnetizing-energy / Coss / dead-time commutation constraints and high-resolution PFM / HRPWM timing. Its 360–540 V DC bus, 40–60 V battery, 9:1 turns ratio, 165–350 kHz controller range, component values and forced-air cooling are specific to that design and are not transferable to PEBB-LV-01 without redesign.

## New bidirectional converter benchmarks and physics models

31. Lee, Chung, Ryu and Ha, “Design Methodology for a CLLLC Resonant Dual Active Bridge Converter With Minimized RMS Current and ZVS Operation for Predefined EV Charging Profile,” *IEEE Access*, Volume 14, pp. 82018–82035, 29 May 2026, DOI [10.1109/ACCESS.2026.3698097](https://doi.org/10.1109/ACCESS.2026.3698097). Reports a 7.3 kW fixed-frequency 100 kHz CLLLC-RDAB prototype, 400 V fixed input and 200–500 V output, with 97.1–97.8% efficiency over the full charging profile and nearly identical forward/reverse efficiency. The optimization selects TPS parameters to maintain ZVS and minimize RMS current across the predefined profile. This is a powerful high-power research reference, not a matched 500 W / 36–60 V product.
32. Ren, Zhang, Wang, Yu, Sun and Chen, “A Multidimensional Design for Dual Active Bridge Converters in Low-Voltage DC Systems,” *IEEE Transactions on Industrial Electronics*, 73(2), pp. 2264–2275, published February 2026, DOI [10.1109/TIE.2025.3603045](https://doi.org/10.1109/TIE.2025.3603045). Proposes multi-objective particle-swarm optimization plus TPS across ZVS range, power-rating utilization and reactive power; a 1 kW prototype reports peak efficiency above 95.9%, improved ZVS capability, rating utilization and reduced reactive power. This is a useful multi-objective DAB design reference, not an equivalent 500 W CLLC performance comparison.
33. Mahmoud, Abdelsalam, Marei and Ibrahim, “Physics-Informed Operating Region Design of Dual Active Bridge Converters Under Thermal and ZVS Constraints for Spacecraft Electrical Power Systems,” *Engineering Proceedings*, 142(1), 20, available 7 September 2026, DOI [10.3390/engproc2026142020](https://doi.org/10.3390/engproc2026142020). Derives a lossless SPS piecewise-current model, closed-form RMS current, a switching-current-polarity ZVS boundary and a simple lumped loss/thermal operating map. Reports <1.3% closed-form RMS discrepancy against its own time-domain numerical solution and up to 27.3% FHA power overestimation near 90°. Its work is analytical/numerical, not physical hardware validation; its spacecraft baseline parameters are not PEBB-LV-01.

## Reading and interpretation cautions

- The 98.55% SST paper result is a peak result for a specific experimental topology and case study. It is not directly comparable to a different voltage, power, cooling, isolation and measurement boundary.
- ABB product values are manufacturer specifications, not independent head-to-head lab measurements.
- ESIG / NLR grid-forming work applies to later AC/microgrid stages, not the first DC/DC demonstrator.
- Standards must be purchased/read in full and applicability confirmed by a qualified compliance specialist. Abstracts, catalog pages and search-result snippets do not establish conformity.
- Recheck standard editions, local utility requirements and product-scope exclusions before any design freeze or certification plan.

## Additional topology and semiconductor research

17. **IEEE Access**, “Design and Experimental Validation of a 25-kW Dual DAB–CLLC Bidirectional DC/DC Converter With an Ultrawide 200–1000 V Operating Range,” published 23 April 2026, pp. 63626–63642, DOI [10.1109/ACCESS.2026.3686835](https://doi.org/10.1109/ACCESS.2026.3686835). Reports 25 kW, 200–1000 V and peak efficiencies of 98.37% charging / 97.82% discharging. The series/parallel reconfigurable module arrangement is an architectural reference; this is not a matched 48 V / 500 W benchmark.
18. **IEEE APEC 2025**, “Switching Performance Comparison of Low-Voltage GaN and Si Devices,” DOI [10.1109/APEC48143.2025.10977390](https://doi.org/10.1109/APEC48143.2025.10977390). Reports lower switching and gate-driver losses for GaN in the tested configuration, alongside sensitivity to dead-time and dynamic RDS(on) behavior at 1 MHz. Treat the quantitative findings as configuration-specific, not universal device multipliers.
19. Sun et al., **Tsinghua University / Chinese Journal of Electrical Engineering**, “Review of Analysis Methods and Control Technologies for CLLC Resonant Converters,” 11(2), pp. 1–16, published 30 June 2025, DOI [10.23919/CJEE.2025.000131](https://doi.org/10.23919/CJEE.2025.000131). Reviews CLLC analysis and control, including soft start, synchronous rectification, dynamic control, bidirectional switching and wide voltage gain.


## Commercial low-voltage converter baselines

20. **ELSİS**, [BIDIR Module — official product page](https://www.elsis.com.tr/en/products/bidir-module). Current public product page lists bidirectional 500 W buck-boost conversion, 9–60 V DC input, 3.3–48 V adjustable output, >97% efficiency, I²C and protection features. The page does not specify galvanic isolation. See also the [two-page product datasheet](https://www.elsis.com.tr/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/BIDIR_DCDC_MODULE_DATASHEET.pdf), which lists >97% at full rated load current, eighth-brick dimensions and 66.2 g weight. These are vendor specifications, not independently reproduced measurements.
21. **Monolithic Power Systems (MPS)**, [48V Datacenter Solutions, Q4 2024 brochure](https://media.monolithicpower.cn/mps_cms_document/d/i/digital-48v_datacenter_solution-q4_2024.pdf), especially pp. 7–9. The MPC10106-54-0500 is described as a continuous 500 W, 40–60 V input, 8:1 non-isolated LLC module; the table lists 2,634 W/in³ and 97.8% peak efficiency. It is a fixed-ratio point-of-load stage, not a bidirectional isolated converter.
22. **Vicor**, [48V Input Maxi Family datasheet](https://www.vicorpower.com/documents/datasheets/ds_48vin-maxi-family.pdf). The V48A48C500 family table lists 500 W, 48 V output and 89.7% typical efficiency at nominal input / 75% load / 25 °C. The associated family is a unidirectional isolated module, not a bidirectional match. Distributor listings identify 3 kV isolation and 36–75 V input for the relevant 500 W 48 V SKU; verify exact revision and SKU against the official datasheet before using in a direct test.
